The Operating Plan: The Booked-Job Desk

This is the one page you run the business from. The AI Agency Blueprint is now a reference library: use it for how-to detail (cold email setup, referral partners, legal), never for what to sell or what to do next. Where the two disagree, this page wins.

The plan in one minute

What we say

“We turn the customers and estimates you already have into booked jobs, and you only pay for jobs that land on your schedule.”

We run the revenue tools contractors already pay for and don’t use: win back past customers, chase open estimates, ask for reviews. AI drafts, a bilingual person checks every message, and the client’s own system sends it. The proof is their own job board.

Who

US HVAC, plumbing, electrical and roofing shops at ~$1-3M revenue (30-400 Google reviews), already on Jobber or Housecall Pro, with 1,000+ past customers. Mountain/Pacific first, plus Spanish-serving shops (Paola’s list).

How we get paid

$0 to start. Pilot: $75 per booked job. Then $400/month + $40 per booked job, capped at $1,500. No setup fee.

Why it works from Germany

Nothing is live or real-time. You build in your own hours, sends are scheduled for US mornings, and replies go straight to the client’s office. No night shifts, and no racing a $29 phone tool.

Time it needs

Minimum 4-6 hours a week each to get going. Each live client then takes about 3 hours a month in total (Mike ~1.5, Paola ~1.5), so 10 clients is ~8 hours a week combined.

Why we changed course (and what we kept)

Dropped as the core

  • Selling the overnight phone. AI answering is a $15-29 feature inside Jobber, Housecall Pro, Zoom and Quo. Managed rivals list $297-789/mo, Housecall Pro adds human backup (HCP Assist), and Smith.ai sells AI plus humans.
  • The Morning Board as the product. The tools already summarise every call. Paola’s CET morning is the US night, so she can tag calls but not act on them. The owner still does the work. Now an add-on.
  • “Google’s AI is calling you” urgency. It has been live since July, every vendor says it, and businesses can opt out.
  • The $1,000 floor, and building a custom voice agent before anyone pays.
  • The 11,000-line course as a to-do list. Four competing calendars is too much for two part-timers.

Kept

  • The niche and the buyer: owner-run contractors at $1-3M.
  • Price against a hire or a result, never against software.
  • Paola’s Spanish, now used where it matters most: Spanish-language campaigns and customer replies.
  • Mountain/Pacific targeting. Your CET evening is their morning, which suits sales calls.
  • No outbound on agency-owned phone numbers. The client’s systems send.
  • AIGrowthEngine for finding and qualifying prospects.

Why this should still work in 2027

  1. The methods are older than AI. Win-back campaigns, estimate follow-up and review requests worked before ChatGPT and will work after whichever AI vendor wins. AI only makes them cheap enough to run for a 5-truck shop.
  2. We sit on top of the client’s software, not against it. Jobber already ships email Campaigns with segments and an AI campaign generator. Every feature a platform releases is more for us to run, not a competitor. Contractors pay for these tools and rarely use them.
  3. Outcome pricing survives commoditisation. If drafting gets cheaper, our cost falls and the price, set per booked job, holds.
  4. Reviews compound. Google’s AI caller only reaches shops that rank locally, and lead marketplaces are watching AI-assistant referrals as a new channel. A steady stream of fresh reviews feeds both.
  5. Low regulatory exposure. Email to the client’s own customers with an unsubscribe link, and texts only to customers with documented consent, sent from the client’s own system. No cold calls or texts from us to consumers, ever.

Honest caveat: the published reactivation results are vendor claims. This plan is a hypothesis with a 60-day test built in (see Gates). It is a cheaper test than the old plan’s, because a pilot costs nobody anything up front.

The 90-day plan

Day 1 is whenever you choose. Write it here: · Ticks are saved in this browser only.

Before Day 1: one evening together (~2 hours)

Weeks 1-2: build the kit on fake data

Done when you can run the whole loop on a 200-row sample customer file in under 2 hours.

Weeks 3-4: first conversations, first pilots

Done when 2 pilots are agreed. Volume each week: 20 warm asks, 50 cold touches, 2 referral-partner conversations.

Weeks 5-8: deliver, prove, convert

Done when the first invoice is paid and the first pilot is converted to the Desk.

Weeks 9-12: repeat and tighten

Done when 3 or more clients are paying for the Desk.

Gates: decide on evidence, not hope

  • Day 30: fewer than 2 pilots agreed after ~200 touches means the offer or the list is wrong. Test the free Count with 10 more owners, and ask why they said no.
  • Day 60: no pilot booked 5 or more jobs? Check the list (too small? too recent?) and the channel (email only? sent in their slow month?). Run one more campaign before changing anything else.
  • Day 90: fewer than 3 Desk clients? Stop and decide together: switch the Desk to a flat $500/mo, narrow to Spanish-serving shops only, or pause. Don’t spend a second quarter defending a plan the market answered.

Rules we don’t break

  • Only the client’s systems send, to the client’s own customers. No purchased lists, ever.
  • Email: authenticated domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), one-click unsubscribe, honour opt-outs immediately, and the client’s real address in the footer.
  • Texts: only to customers with documented consent; send 8am-9pm in the customer’s local time; honour STOP. For customers in Florida or Oklahoma, use email only unless written consent is on file (those states’ laws carry per-message penalties). Ask a lawyer before any client wants more than this.
  • The client approves every send. We never quote job prices or promise anything on their behalf that they haven’t written down.
  • No agency-owned consumer phone numbers, and no cold calls or texts to consumers. Our own cold outreach goes only to business owners.
  • Every AI-drafted message is reviewed by a person before it sends.
