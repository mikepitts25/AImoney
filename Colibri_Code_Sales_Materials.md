# 1. COLD CALL SCRIPT (Mike)

## BEFORE YOU DIAL -- Quick Prep (2 minutes)

- Paola's **Live Test** log for this prospect: the timestamp she called after hours, and what happened (voicemail? AI? which one? did it give a usable answer?).
- Their Google Business Profile: rating, review count (30-400 is the ICP band), hours, whether services/price ranges/service area are filled in.
- **Which AI answered, if any.** Jobber, Housecall Pro, Zoom and Quo greetings are recognisable. This decides the pitch: "no AI" → we deploy ours; "has AI" → we run theirs.
- Owner's first name.

Dial windows (Mike's real calendar): Tue + Thu 18:00-19:30 CET, Saturday 15:00-17:00 CET. 50 dials/week.

## SCENARIO A: THEY ANSWER

### Opening (first 10 seconds) -- lead with the timestamp, not the product

> **Mike:** Hey [First Name], it's Mike with Colibri Code. I'll be quick -- I called your line at [7:14pm last Tuesday] and wanted to tell you what happened. Got thirty seconds?

**Why this works:** it's first-party evidence about *their* business, not a statistic, and it sounds like news rather than a pitch.

### What happened (20 seconds) -- pick the branch that matches Paola's log

**If it went to voicemail:**
> **Mike:** It rang out to voicemail. Which is normal -- most shops your size do that after five. The thing is, Google stopped keeping your call history this year, so unless someone listens to every voicemail, you've got no record those people ever called. How are you handling evenings right now?

**If their AI picked up:**
> **Mike:** Your AI picked up -- [Jobber's / Zoom's], sounded like. Did a decent job taking a message. My question is: who reads what it did overnight? Because when I asked it [rough price for an AC repair, earliest slot], it [couldn't say / said someone would call back]. Is anyone checking those conversations before your guys head out in the morning?

**If a human answering service picked up:**
> **Mike:** A service picked up -- took my name and number. Do you get those as a list in the morning, or as a pile of texts? And does anyone call the Spanish speakers back?

### The offer (20 seconds)

> **Mike:** So here's what we do. Whatever answers your phone at night -- yours or ours -- my business partner Paola reads every conversation before you're up, fixes what it got wrong, calls the Spanish speakers back herself, and hands you a sorted list: book today, call back, quote sent, spam. You open that with your coffee instead of a voicemail pile. I'd love to show you what one of those mornings looks like -- 20 minutes. Tuesday or Thursday better?

### If They Say Yes

> **Mike:** Great. What's the best email for the invite? ... Perfect. Before we talk I'll send you a sample board so you know what you're looking at.

### "I already have a receptionist / answering service."
> **Mike:** Great. Does she cover nights and weekends? ... And the service -- do you get a sorted list, or a stack of messages? We sit behind both: we don't replace anyone, we make sure nothing from the night gets lost before your team is in.

### "Just send me an email."

**Key:** always get the email, always send the recording + sample board.

# 2. DISCOVERY / DEMO CALL SCRIPT (20 Minutes)
# =============================================================

## PRE-CALL PREP

- Paola's Live Test log + the recording.
- Their Business Profile, read the way Google's AI would read it: services, price ranges, hours, service area. Note what's missing.
- Which answering tool they use (from the Live Test).
- A **Morning Board** ready to share: from the demo line before client one; a real one, names blurred, after.

## MINUTE 0-2: Rapport + agenda

> **Mike:** Thanks for making time. Quick agenda: I'll ask a few questions about how evenings work for you, show you what a morning looks like with us running the desk, and then we'll see if it makes sense. Fair?

## MINUTE 2-7: Discovery (ask like a conversation)

1. **"Who answers your phone after 5 and on weekends right now?"**
2. **"Do you pay for any answering tool today -- AI or human?"** *(Decides BYO vs ours.)*
3. **"When you get in the morning, how do you find out who called overnight?"** *(Usually: "I listen to voicemails" or "I scroll the app." This is the pain.)*
4. **"How many of those overnight people end up booked? How do you know?"**
5. **"What's a typical repair ticket for you? And an install?"** *(Their number anchors the value conversation.)*
6. **"Do you get Spanish-speaking callers? Who talks to them?"**
7. **"Have you heard Google's AI is starting to phone contractors for quotes on homeowners' behalf?"** *(Then show what their profile/line would give it.)*
8. **"What would have to be true for you to pay someone to own this?"**

### Transition
> **Mike:** So right now, evenings land on [voicemail / the Jobber AI], and in the morning someone [listens through them / scrolls the app] -- and nobody checks the Spanish ones. Let me show you what that same night looks like when someone's running it.

## MINUTE 7-13: Show the Morning Board (this is the close -- not the AI)

1. **Show the board.** "Every call and text after 5, sorted: book today, call back, quote sent, spam. Paola read every one before 7 your time."
2. **Show a correction.** "Here the AI got the address wrong -- she fixed it and flagged it."
3. **Show a Spanish callback.** "Here she called the customer back herself and booked it."
4. **Show an escalation.** "This one was a real emergency -- it went to your on-call tech live, it didn't wait for the board."
5. **The call-log line.** "Google stopped keeping your call history this year -- this is your record now."
6. **Only now, the answering layer.** If BYO: "We run this on the Jobber AI you already have -- nothing to switch." If ours: play a 60-second call from the demo line.

## MINUTE 13-16: Price -- against a hire

> **Mike:** Compare it to a hire, not software. A night CSR is $3,300 to $4,600 a month loaded and goes home at 2am. The Front Office is [price from the ladder] a month with a $2,500 setup -- overnight answering, Paola's board every morning, follow-up texts and review requests. At your [$Z] ticket, that's about [price ÷ Z] jobs a month to cover it. If you'd rather see it first, there's a 7-day paid pilot for $250, credited to setup.

### "Let me think about it"
> **Mike:** Of course. What's the part you'd want to think through -- the price, or whether it'll actually work for your shop? *(If "work": offer the $250 pilot. If "price": go back to the hire comparison; never discount the monthly -- waive setup if you must.)*

## MINUTE 16-20: Close or next step

**Ready:**
> **Mike:** I'll send the agreement today. Paola runs onboarding -- a 30-minute call to get your services, service area, dispatch rules and on-call number. You get your first board within a week.

**Partner/spouse:**
> **Mike:** Makes sense. I'll send a summary, the recording from your line and the sample board so you can show [them]. Can we pencil [specific day] to answer questions?

**Always set a specific follow-up date.**

## PRICING PRESENTATION FRAMEWORK

1. **Anchor on the hire.** "A night CSR is $3,300-4,600 a month loaded."
2. **Name the gap.** "Answering services take messages. AI tools take messages. Nobody reads them before you're up."
3. **Present the Front Office.** Price from the ladder. Two options only: Bundle and Scale. Pilot for anyone who wants to try first.
4. **Break-even on their ticket.** "At your [$Z] ticket, that's [price ÷ Z] jobs a month."

## DISCOUNT RULES

- **Never discount the monthly.** It compounds. Waive or reduce the setup fee instead.
- Waive setup for: clients 1-3 (in exchange for a written case study + permission to use board screenshots), referrals from existing clients.
- **No annual prepay discounts** until you've retained clients past month 6 -- you don't yet know your churn.
- **Grandfather rule:** existing clients keep their rate forever.
- **No per-minute overage line.** With our AI, the Bundle includes 1,500 minutes; anything above is pass-through at cost plus a stated handling fee, never a profit line. With BYO, minutes are the client's own tool's business.
- **Never say "unlimited."**

# 5. VALUE TALKING POINTS (replaces the old ROI calculator)
# =============================================================

The old calculator multiplied "industry average" missed-call rates by job values and produced $36,800/month "lost revenue" figures. Owners don't believe them, and they shouldn't. Use three honest frames instead, in this order.

## Frame 1: Compare to a hire

> "A night or weekend CSR is $3,300-4,600 a month fully loaded. We're [price]. And she'd go home at 2am; the board covers the whole night."

## Frame 2: Break-even on *their* ticket (revenue needed to cover the fee)

| Monthly fee | $300 ticket | $450 ticket | $800 ticket | $1,500 ticket | $11,000 roof |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $650 (Branch B) | 2.2 jobs | 1.4 jobs | 0.8 jobs | 0.4 jobs | 0.06 jobs |
| $1,000 | 3.3 jobs | 2.2 jobs | 1.25 jobs | 0.7 jobs | 0.09 jobs |

Be straight about it: this is **revenue** to cover the fee. On the owner's gross margin it's roughly double the job count. Say that out loud -- it's why they'll trust the rest.

## Frame 3: Their own numbers after 30 days

> "Don't believe any number I give you today. After 30 days, the board tells you exactly how many overnight conversations came in, how many booked, and what the AI got wrong. If it's not worth it, you'll see that too."

The monthly report (Paola) pulls booked jobs from the client's own job board -- never from our system's guesses.
