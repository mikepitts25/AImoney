Tax Situation: What You Need to Know

Your LLC is a US company, serving US clients, paid through US banks. You owe US taxes — that part is simple. The Germany question is more nuanced. Below we break down what the law says, what actually happens in practice, and our recommended approach.

1. What the Law Technically Says

SOFA exempts your military/government pay from German tax. NATO SOFA Article X is crystal clear on this — Mike's government salary is not taxable by Germany. Period.

Private business income is a gray area. Article X has a carve-out saying Germany can tax "any profitable enterprise other than his employment" that a SOFA member engages in. Army JAG offices and the Ramstein AFB tax guidance interpret this to mean that side business income could be subject to German taxation, because the work is physically performed in Germany.

The Gewerbeanmeldung question: Stuttgart's Spousal Employment Guide says profitable activities "generally require" trade registration. The Gewerbeamt determines if it's needed based on the type of activity.

Sources: NATO SOFA Article X, Ramstein AFB Taxation Guidance (Aug 2023), US Army Garrison Stuttgart Spousal Employment Guide, Kaiserslautern American

2. What Actually Happens (Enforcement Reality)

The Finanzamt has zero visibility into your US business. Your Wyoming LLC, US bank account, Stripe payments, and US tax filings are completely invisible to German tax authorities. There is no information-sharing mechanism between the IRS and the Finanzamt for LLC pass-through income.

Thousands of SOFA families run side businesses without registering in Germany. Etsy shops, freelance work, online coaching, MLMs, Amazon FBA, software development — the military communities are full of spouse-run and dual-income businesses operating through US entities. Enforcement against these is essentially nonexistent.

Your specific situation is especially low-risk:

  • • US LLC registered in a US state
  • • US-only clients (no German customers)
  • • US bank account and payment processing
  • • No physical presence in German commerce (no German ads, no German invoices)
  • • No German employees or contractors
  • • No German business bank account

When would risk increase? Taking German clients, opening a German business bank account, advertising in Germany, hiring German workers, or having someone report you to the Finanzamt. As long as the business stays 100% US-facing, the practical risk is very low.

3. What You Definitely Owe (US Taxes)

Self-employment tax: 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on net self-employment income up to $168,600 (2026). This applies to both Mike and Paola's share of LLC profits. This is the biggest tax bite and there is no way around it.

Federal income tax: 10-37% progressive rates. At $50K net profit each (50/50 split), your effective federal rate is approximately 12-15%. At $100K each, approximately 18-22%.

FEIE (Foreign Earned Income Exclusion): May exclude up to ~$130,000 of earned income from federal income tax — potentially zeroing out your federal liability. FEIE does NOT reduce self-employment tax. SOFA eligibility is debated among tax pros, but many SOFA families successfully claim it using the Physical Presence Test (330 days outside the US). Worth discussing with a CPA.

State taxes: If LLC is registered in Wyoming, Nevada, or South Dakota — no state income tax. Register in a no-income-tax state.

Estimated quarterly payments: The IRS expects quarterly estimated tax payments (April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15). Use Form 1040-ES.

5. Important Filings

Form 5472: Required if the LLC has a "foreign" owner. If Paola is not a US person for tax purposes, this is mandatory. Penalty for missing it: $25,000. If she elects to file jointly with Mike as a US resident, you may not need it. Clarify this with your CPA first.

Form 8938 (FATCA): Required if foreign financial assets exceed $200,000 (filing jointly abroad). May apply depending on your German bank balances.

6. One Rule You Cannot Break

Never use SOFA privileges for business purposes. This is the one thing that can actually get you in trouble, regardless of the German tax question:

  • • No APO/FPO address for business mail or LLC registration
  • • No USAREUR-plated vehicle for business activities
  • • No tax-free goods (PX/Commissary/VAT-form purchases) used for business
  • • No military installation facilities for private business without authorization

Violations can result in loss of SOFA privileges — which would be catastrophic. Use your US home address (virtual address via iPostal1) and keep everything completely separate.

What Happens When SOFA Ends

If Mike leaves his SOFA-covered position, you become regular German tax residents within 90 days. At that point, German taxes on the LLC become mandatory — not optional. Plan 3-6 months ahead:

  • • German income tax on worldwide income (14-45% progressive)
  • • Gewerbeanmeldung required for the LLC's activities
  • • Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, ~14-17% with €24,500 exemption)
  • • US-Germany tax treaty + Foreign Tax Credit prevent paying double
  • • Consider relocating the business or restructuring before SOFA ends
  • • Hire a Steuerberater with US-German cross-border experience early.

Plain English: What Each Tool Actually Does

Vapi = the "voice" of the AI phone agent. It's the service that actually answers the phone and talks to callers. Think of it as the mouth and ears.

Twilio = the phone company. It gives you real phone numbers and sends/receives texts. Think of it as the phone line Vapi talks through.

n8n = the "brain" that connects everything. When a call ends, n8n takes the info and automatically does things with it — saves the lead, sends a text, emails the client. It's like a digital assistant that follows rules you set up. No code needed — you drag and drop boxes on screen.

Claude API / OpenAI = the intelligence behind the voice. This is what makes the AI agent actually understand what the caller is saying and respond smartly — not just play pre-recorded messages.

Supabase = a spreadsheet on steroids. It stores all your data — every call, every lead, every client. You can pull reports from it. Free for your scale.

Hetzner VPS = a rented computer that runs 24/7 in the cloud. It's where n8n lives. You'll never see it physically — you access it through your browser. $5/month.

Smartlead = an email sending tool designed for outreach. It sends your cold emails, warms up your email addresses so they don't go to spam, and tracks who opens/replies.

LLM = "Large Language Model" — the AI that understands language. Claude and GPT-4 are LLMs. When we say "LLM cost" we mean the cost of the AI thinking during calls.

TTS / STT = "Text to Speech" / "Speech to Text." TTS is how the AI turns its written response into a voice. STT is how it understands what the caller said. Both happen inside Vapi automatically.

Webhook = an automatic notification between tools. When a call ends in Vapi, it sends a webhook (a little data packet) to n8n saying "hey, a call just happened, here's the info." n8n then does something with it. You set this up once and it runs forever.

API key = a password that lets one tool talk to another. When Mike sets up Vapi to use Claude, he copies an API key from Anthropic into Vapi. It's how tools authenticate with each other.

Discovery Call Framework: Qualify, Diagnose, Close

A structured 17-minute call that qualifies the prospect, diagnoses their pain, and closes the trial. Follow this framework every time — consistency beats improvisation.

Pre-Call Preparation Checklist

Do these 5 minutes before every discovery call. Walking in prepared earns instant credibility.

Call their business number first. Does it go to voicemail? How many rings? What does the voicemail say? Screenshot the result — you will use this on the call.

Check their Google reviews. How many? What’s the rating? Any complaints about “couldn’t reach anyone” or “nobody called back”? Screenshot these.

Look at their Google Business Profile. Are their hours listed? Do they have a website? What services do they advertise?

Check their website (if any). Do they have a contact form? Live chat? Or just a phone number? Note how they currently capture leads.

Prepare your demo number. Make sure your demo agent is live and working. Test-call it once before the meeting.

The 5-Phase Call Structure (17 Minutes)

Phase 1: Rapport & Intro (2 min)

Thank them for their time. Briefly explain what you do in one sentence. Set the agenda: “I’d love to ask a few questions about your business, then show you something cool, and we’ll see if it makes sense. Sound good?”

Key: Be warm, be human, don’t pitch yet. Contractors respect people who get to the point without being pushy.

Phase 2: Problem Diagnosis (8 min)

This is where you earn the right to sell. Ask these questions and listen. Take notes. Their answers become your pitch.

“How many calls does your business get per day, roughly?”

“How many of those go to voicemail or get missed?”

“What happens with after-hours calls right now?”

“What’s your average job value?”

“Have you lost a job recently because of a missed call?”

“Do you have a receptionist or office manager answering phones?”

“What happens on weekends — do calls go anywhere?”

“If a customer calls and nobody answers, what do they do?”

Pro tip: When they say “yeah we miss a lot of calls,” ask “how many per week, would you guess?” Get a number. You need their numbers for Phase 3.

Phase 3: Value Presentation (3 min)

Mirror their own numbers back to them. This is not a generic pitch — it’s their specific situation.

Example: “So you said you miss about 5 calls a week, and your average job is $3,000. That’s potentially $15,000 a week walking to your competitors. Even if only 20% of those would have booked, that’s $3,000/week or $12,000/month in lost revenue. Our service costs $1,000/month. The math kind of speaks for itself.”

Key: Use THEIR numbers, not yours. Let the ROI math do the selling.

Phase 4: Live Demo (2 min)

“Let me show you what this actually sounds like. I’m going to call our demo number right now — pretend you’re a homeowner with a broken AC.”

Call the demo number on speaker. Let the AI handle it. This is always the “wow” moment. Don’t talk over it — let them hear it work.

After the call: “Imagine that answering every call to your business, 24/7, and texting you the details instantly.”

Red Flags: When to Walk Away

Not every prospect is a good fit. Walking away from bad clients saves you months of headaches. Watch for these:

Budget under $300/mo: They can’t afford the service and will churn fast. Politely redirect to a DIY solution.

Wants to “think about it” for more than 2 weeks: They’re not in pain. Follow up once, then move on.

Wants you to guarantee a specific number of calls: You can’t control how many people call their business. You guarantee every call gets answered.

Wants custom development for free: “Can you also build us a website and CRM?” Scope creep kills profitability. Offer it as a paid add-on.

Abusive or disrespectful: Trust your gut. If they’re rude on the sales call, they’ll be worse as a client.

Option A: Call Forwarding (Recommended to Start)

How it works: Client keeps their existing number. They set up conditional call forwarding (on no-answer, busy, or all calls) to your Twilio AI number.

Pros: Zero risk for client. Done in 5 minutes. They keep their number. Easy to revert.

Cons: Slight delay (1-2 extra rings before forwarding). Some carriers charge for forwarding.

Setup steps:

  1. Buy a Twilio number in client's area code
  2. Configure Vapi to use that Twilio number as its inbound line
  3. Client activates forwarding: iPhone: Settings → Phone → Call Forwarding. Android: Phone app → Settings → Call Forwarding. Or dial *72 + [your Twilio number] on a landline.
  4. Test with a call — should ring to AI after client doesn't answer

Best for: Every new client. Start here. Switch to porting only if they want to fully hand over call management.

Option B: Full Number Porting

How it works: Client's existing business number is ported to Twilio. All calls go directly to your AI agent. You control everything.

Pros: No forwarding delay. Full control. Cleaner setup. Better call quality.

Cons: Takes 1-3 weeks to port. Client must authorize. If they leave you, they need to port back.

When to use: Month 2+ clients who are committed. Enterprise plans. Clients who want you managing everything.

Setup: Submit LOA (Letter of Authorization) through Twilio. Provide: business name, authorized contact, account number with current carrier, PIN if applicable.

Option C: New Number (Simplest)

How it works: You give the client a new number that's answered by AI. They advertise it as their "24/7 service line" alongside their existing number.

Pros: Instant setup. No risk. Client can test without changing anything.

Cons: Doesn't capture calls to their existing number. Requires them to promote a new number.

Best for: Free trials, demo phase, or as an "after-hours line" alongside their main number.

A2P 10DLC SMS Compliance (Mandatory)

If you send SMS to clients' customers (confirmations, follow-ups, review requests), you must register for A2P 10DLC. Without it, carriers will block your messages.

What it is: "Application-to-Person 10-Digit Long Code" — the US carrier registration system for business SMS.

What you register:

  • • Brand: Register Colibri Code LLC ($4 one-time for sole proprietor, $4-48 for standard)

Timeline: 2-4 weeks for approval. Start this on Day 1 — don't wait until you have a client.

Per-client: You can use one campaign for multiple clients if they're the same use case. Or register the client's own brand if they prefer.

Without registration: Messages get filtered/blocked. Deliverability drops to 20-40%. Fines possible.

Action: Register your Twilio brand + campaign in Week 1. It takes time to approve and you can't send SMS without it. Do NOT skip this.

How to Build Agentic Workflows (Technical)

The core pattern for every agentic AI service is the same. Once you build one, the others are variations on the same architecture.

The Agent Loop Pattern

Trigger → Gather Context → LLM Decides Action → Execute Action → Observe Result → LLM Decides Next Action → Loop Until Done

Implementation in your stack:

• n8n “AI Agent” node: Built-in support for the agent loop pattern. Give Claude tools (Supabase query, Twilio send, Calendar check, etc.) and a goal. It reasons about which tool to use and when.

• Claude tool use (function calling): Define your tools as JSON schemas. Claude receives the context, decides which tool(s) to call, you execute them, feed results back, Claude decides next step. This is the core of agentic behavior.

• Supabase for memory: Store conversation history, customer profiles, and action logs. The agent reads history before deciding. This gives it long-term memory across interactions.

• n8n cron for proactive triggers: Not all agents are triggered by a call. The Maintenance Plan agent runs daily at 9am, checks which customers need outreach, and acts. The Quote Follow-Up agent runs every 2 hours and checks for stale quotes.

Backup, Security & Access Management

Your clients trust you with their business communications. One leaked API key or lost workflow can cost you a client and your reputation. Lock everything down from Day 1.

API Key Management

Use a password manager: Store every API key, password, and credential in Bitwarden (free) or 1Password. Never in plain text, never in a Google Doc, never in a Slack message.

Never put keys in n8n node names or workflow comments. Anyone with access to your n8n instance can see these. Use n8n’s built-in credential store instead.

Use n8n credentials properly: Go to Settings → Credentials → Create New. Store Supabase, Twilio, Vapi, and email API keys here. Reference them in nodes — don’t paste keys directly into HTTP request nodes.

Separate keys per service where possible. Don’t reuse the same API key across different integrations. If one is compromised, only that integration is affected.

2FA Checklist

Enable two-factor authentication on every account. No exceptions. Use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy), not SMS.

Backup Procedures

n8n workflows (weekly): Settings → Export All Workflows. Save the JSON file to Google Drive or a password-protected folder. Do this every Sunday night. One lost workflow = hours of rebuilding.

Supabase (auto on paid, manual on free): Paid plans have daily auto-backups with point-in-time recovery. On the free tier: monthly, go to SQL Editor and run pg_dump or use the Supabase CLI to export your data. Save the SQL file.

Vapi agent configs: Document each agent’s system prompt, voice settings, and tool configurations in a shared Google Doc. If Vapi ever resets or you need to rebuild, you have the blueprint.

Client data: Export Supabase client table as CSV quarterly. Store in a folder: /backups/[YEAR-MONTH]/clients.csv

Automation idea: Build an n8n workflow that auto-exports your Supabase data on the 1st of each month and emails you the CSV. Takes 30 minutes to build, saves you from ever forgetting.

Access Control

Never share master credentials. If Paola needs Twilio access, create a sub-user with limited permissions. Same for n8n — create a separate user account.

Create sub-accounts where possible: Twilio supports sub-accounts per client. Supabase lets you create separate projects. This keeps client data isolated.

Rotate API keys quarterly. Set a calendar reminder for every 3 months. Generate new keys, update your credential store and all workflows, then revoke the old keys.

If an API Key Is Compromised

Act immediately. Don’t wait — every minute a compromised key is live is a risk.

Step 1: Revoke the compromised key immediately in the provider’s dashboard.

Step 2: Generate a new key from the same provider.

Step 3: Update every n8n workflow and credential that used the old key.

Step 4: Audit recent API calls in the provider’s logs. Look for unusual activity — calls you didn’t make, data exports, new resources created.

Step 5: If client data was potentially exposed, notify affected clients within 24 hours. Transparency builds trust even in bad situations.

VPS Security Basics

Your Hetzner VPS runs n8n and potentially other services. Lock it down.

SSH key only: Disable password authentication. Use SSH key pairs. Add your key during Hetzner server creation, then edit /etc/ssh/sshd_config and set PasswordAuthentication no.

Firewall: Allow only ports 80 (HTTP), 443 (HTTPS), and your SSH port. Block everything else. Use ufw on Ubuntu:

sudo ufw default deny incoming sudo ufw default allow outgoing sudo ufw allow 22/tcp # SSH sudo ufw allow 80/tcp # HTTP sudo ufw allow 443/tcp # HTTPS sudo ufw enable

Automatic security updates: Enable unattended upgrades so critical patches install automatically:

sudo apt install unattended-upgrades sudo dpkg-reconfigure -plow unattended-upgrades

Keep Docker updated: Run sudo apt update && sudo apt upgrade monthly. Check for n8n Docker image updates: docker pull n8nio/n8n:latest then restart the container.

Technical Troubleshooting Guide

When something breaks (and it will), don’t panic. Find the symptom below, follow the fix steps, and you’ll be back online in minutes.

1. AI agent sounds robotic or has long pauses

Check Vapi latency settings: Dashboard → Agent → Advanced → ensure “Response Delay” is under 800ms.

Try a different TTS voice: Some ElevenLabs voices have more natural cadence. Test “Rachel,” “Josh,” or “Bella.”

Verify LLM selection: Use Haiku for speed-critical responses, Sonnet for quality-critical conversations. Opus is overkill and adds latency.

Check VPS CPU usage: SSH in and run htop. If CPU is pegged at 100%, your VPS is too small or something else is consuming resources.

2. n8n workflow isn’t triggering

Check webhook URL: Is the URL in Vapi/Twilio pointing to your current n8n instance? Copy it from the webhook node in n8n and compare.

Verify n8n is running: SSH in and run docker ps. If n8n container isn’t listed, restart it with docker-compose up -d.

Check workflow is Active: In n8n, the toggle in the top-right must be ON (green). Inactive workflows ignore all triggers.

Review execution log: n8n → Executions (left sidebar). Look for red (failed) executions. Click to see the error message and which node failed.

Check if n8n is publicly reachable: Try opening your n8n webhook URL in a browser. If you get a timeout, your VPS firewall or DNS might be misconfigured.

3. SMS confirmations not sending

Verify Twilio credentials in n8n: Settings → Credentials → Twilio. Test the connection. Re-enter Account SID and Auth Token if needed.

Check A2P 10DLC registration status: Twilio Console → Messaging → Trust Hub. If your campaign is “Pending” or “Rejected,” SMS will be blocked.

Confirm Twilio account balance: If your Twilio balance hits $0, all messaging stops. Set up auto-recharge in Billing.

Check phone number capability: Twilio Console → Phone Numbers → click the number → verify it’s SMS-enabled (not all numbers are).

4. Supabase connection errors

Check API key hasn’t expired: Supabase → Settings → API. Copy the anon key and update it in n8n credentials if needed.

Verify RLS policies: If you enabled Row Level Security but didn’t create policies, all queries will return empty results. Check Tables → Policies.

Check free tier limits: Free tier gives you 500MB storage and limited connections. If you’re near the limit, consider upgrading ($25/mo) or cleaning old data.

Test connection independently: In n8n, create a simple workflow with a Supabase node that reads one row. If this works, the issue is in your specific workflow logic.

5. Call recordings not saving

Verify Vapi recording settings: Agent → Advanced Settings → ensure “Record Calls” is enabled.

Check webhook payload: In n8n, look at the incoming webhook data. Is recording_url present? If not, the call may have been too short or recording failed.

Verify Supabase column exists: Check that your calls table has a recording_url column of type TEXT. If it’s missing, add it via SQL Editor.

Check timing: Vapi recordings take 30-60 seconds after the call ends to process. Your webhook might fire before the recording URL is available. Add a “Wait” node (60s) before saving.

6. Agent answers but doesn’t book appointments

Check Cal.com integration: Verify the Cal.com API key in your n8n workflow is valid. Test by manually creating a booking via the API.

Verify calendar has available slots: Log into Cal.com → Availability. If all slots are blocked, the agent can’t book anything.

Test the booking webhook independently: Trigger the booking node in n8n manually with test data. Check if the error is in the Cal.com node or upstream.

Check the agent prompt: Make sure the system prompt tells the AI to collect the right data (name, phone, address, preferred time) before attempting to book.

7. Client says calls are going to voicemail instead of AI

Verify call forwarding: Call the client’s business number yourself. Does it ring and then forward, or go straight to voicemail? If voicemail, forwarding isn’t set up correctly.

Test the Twilio number directly: Call the Twilio number (not the client’s number). If the AI answers, the issue is forwarding, not your setup.

Check Twilio call logs: Console → Monitor → Logs → Calls. Are incoming calls showing up? If not, the forwarding isn’t reaching Twilio.

Common fix: Have the client re-activate forwarding. On iPhone: Settings → Phone → Call Forwarding → toggle off, then on, re-enter Twilio number. On landline: dial *73 to cancel, then *72 + Twilio number to re-set.

Satisfaction Guarantee & Refund Policy

A clear guarantee removes the #1 objection (“what if it doesn’t work?”) and closes more deals than any discount. Because you’re month-to-month with a low setup fee, the risk to you is tiny — but the trust it buys is huge. Pick the policy that fits your risk tolerance and state it the same way every time.

Recommended: 14-Day “It Works” Guarantee

“If your agent isn’t answering calls and capturing leads within 14 days of go-live, we’ll refund your setup fee in full — no questions asked.” You keep the first month’s retainer (work was performed); you refund only the one-time setup fee. Low risk, high trust.

Aggressive: 30-Day Money-Back

“Try it for 30 days. If you’re not happy for any reason, cancel and we’ll refund your first month + setup fee.” Closes the most deals but exposes you to ~$1,000+ per refund. Use only once your delivery is dialed in and churn is low.

Policy Rules (Put These in Writing)

Guarantee covers performance, not buyer’s remorse on results you can’t control. You guarantee the agent answers and captures leads — not how many people call their business.

Setup fee refundable, monthly retainer is not (after the period passes). Work performed in a paid month isn’t refunded once that month is delivered.

One guarantee window per client. The window resets only if they cancel and re-sign 6+ months later.

Refunds processed within 7 business days via the original Stripe payment. Document the reason every time — refunds are your most honest churn data.

Data export still provided. Even on a refund, give the client their call recordings and lead data. It costs you nothing and protects your reputation.

SATISFACTION GUARANTEE (add to proposal + contract) We're so confident your AI agent will pay for itself that we back it with a simple promise: If your agent isn't live and answering calls within 14 days of your completed intake, we'll refund your setup fee in full. There's no long-term contract. You're month-to-month and can cancel anytime with 30 days' notice. We earn your business every single month.

Sales Tip: Say It Out Loud

Read the guarantee aloud on the discovery call right before the trial close. It reframes the decision from “should I risk $1,000?” to “what do I have to lose?” Pair it with the month-to-month term and most objections evaporate.

Insurance Checklist

General Liability (GL): ~$50-60/mo through Next Insurance or Hiscox. Covers basic business liability. Get this before your first client.

Errors & Omissions (E&O): ~$40-50/mo. Covers claims of negligence (e.g., AI mishandles a call). Worth having once you pass 3+ clients.

Cyber Liability: Consider at 10+ clients. Covers data breaches and cybersecurity incidents. ~$30-50/mo.

When to get insurance: GL before first client (non-negotiable). E&O by month 3. Cyber liability by month 6+. Total: ~$100-150/mo — already included in the P&L projections.

When Things Go Wrong (And They Will)

Here’s exactly what to do for the 5 most common emergencies. Save this section — you’ll need it.

AI agent says something wrong to a caller

Immediate: Listen to the recording. Identify the exact prompt issue.

Fix: Update the Vapi system prompt to handle that scenario. Test 5 times.

Communicate: Text the client: “We caught an issue on a call and already fixed it. Here’s what happened and what we changed. Won’t happen again.” Proactive transparency builds trust.

n8n or VPS goes down

Immediate: Calls still ring (Vapi handles them), but notifications and logging stop.

Fix: Log into Hetzner console → restart the server. SSH in, run docker-compose up -d. Usually takes 2-5 minutes.

Prevent: Set up UptimeRobot (free, 5-minute checks) to ping your n8n URL. Get email/SMS alerts when it goes down.

Client wants to cancel

Immediate: Call them (don’t text). Ask: “Help me understand what’s not working.” Listen.

Save plays: (1) Offer a free month. (2) Add a free add-on. (3) Reduce to a lower tier instead of full cancel. (4) Pause for 1 month instead of cancel.

If they still cancel: Be gracious. Remove their agent. Send a closing email with their data. Leave the door open: “If things change, we’re here.” They may come back. Or refer someone.

Vapi has an outage

Immediate: Calls should auto-forward to client’s phone (your Twilio fallback). If not set up, configure it NOW.

Communicate: Text affected clients: “Our AI provider is experiencing a brief outage. Calls are forwarding to your phone as a backup. We’ll notify you when it’s restored.”

Monitor: Check status.vapi.ai and Vapi Discord for updates.

A client’s customer complains about “talking to a robot”

Reframe: “Would they prefer to leave a voicemail that never gets returned? The AI ensures every caller gets helped immediately.”

Fix: Listen to the call. Often the issue is a specific phrase or tone. Adjust the prompt to be more natural for that scenario.

Option: Add a “transfer to human” option: “If you’d prefer to speak to someone directly, I can transfer you now.”

Initial Stripe Configuration

Create Stripe account at stripe.com under Colibri Code LLC. Use your EIN, US business address (registered agent), and Mercury bank for payouts.

Set payout schedule to weekly (Settings → Payouts → Payout Schedule). Daily creates noise; monthly leaves you cash-strapped.

Enable Stripe Tax (Products → Tax). Wyoming has no state income tax, but you still need to track revenue for federal. Stripe auto-generates 1099 data.

Add your branding: Logo, brand color, support email (support@colibricode.com). This shows on all invoices and the customer portal.

Enable the Customer Portal (Settings → Customer Portal). Clients can update their own card info when it expires — saves you a support ticket.

Per-Client Billing Setup

Create a Product for each service tier: “AI Phone Agent — Starter ($697/mo)”, “AI Phone Agent — Growth ($997/mo)”, etc. You only create products once; reuse them for every client.

Send a one-time Invoice for the setup fee. Invoices → Create Invoice → add client email → add setup fee line item → Send. They pay with a link.

Create a Subscription for the monthly retainer. Subscriptions → Create → select customer → select product → set billing anchor to the 1st of month. Start date = go-live date.

Enable Smart Retries (Settings → Subscriptions → Manage Failed Payments → Smart Retries ON). Stripe will automatically retry failed charges 3 times over 7 days before marking it failed.

Set dunning emails (Settings → Subscriptions → Manage Failed Payments → Send Emails ON). Stripe emails the client automatically when their card fails. This handles 80% of payment issues without you doing anything.

Failed Payment Automation (n8n)

Stripe’s built-in dunning handles most cases, but for clients who still haven’t paid after retries, automate your escalation.

Webhook trigger: In n8n, create a webhook node listening to Stripe event invoice.payment_failed. Stripe sends this every time a charge fails.

Common Mistakes New Agency Owners Make

Every new agency makes these. Read this list now, bookmark it, and re-read it monthly.

1. Building for 6 months before selling anything

You’ll never feel “ready.” The agent doesn’t need to be perfect — it needs to be better than voicemail. Start selling in Week 3. Fix issues as they come up. The first client teaches you more than 100 hours of building alone.

2. Underpricing because you feel like a beginner

$500/mo is already cheap compared to alternatives (see the competitor pricing table). Don’t go below $500/mo for the core phone agent. If you charge $200/mo, you need 50 clients to make $10K. At $1,000/mo, you need 10. Price for 10.

3. Spending $500+/month on tools before having clients

You do NOT need GoHighLevel ($97/mo), LinkedIn Sales Navigator ($99/mo), Clay ($149/mo), or PhoneBurner ($149/mo) in Month 1. Start with the ultra-lean stack ($16-46). Add tools only when a specific bottleneck demands it.

4. Trying to serve every industry at once

Pick HVAC + Plumbing. Get 5-10 clients. Build case studies. Then expand to electrical, roofing, etc. A case study saying “we helped 8 HVAC companies” is 10x more powerful than “we serve all businesses.”

5. Not setting boundaries with clients

Contractors will text you at 11pm asking for changes. Set expectations from Day 1: “Support hours are 9am-5pm ET. Emergencies (agent down) = we respond within 1 hour anytime. Feature requests = addressed within 48 hours.” Put this in your contract.

6. Forgetting to ask for referrals and reviews

At the 30-day mark, every happy client should be asked: “Know any other contractors who miss calls?” and “Would you mind leaving us a Google review?” Put these in your calendar as recurring tasks. Referrals are your cheapest client acquisition channel.

7. Not monitoring the first 72 hours obsessively

Listen to EVERY call recording for the first 3 days after a client goes live. The AI will say something weird — guaranteed. Catch it before the client does. After 72 hours of clean calls, you can relax to spot-checking.

8. Getting discouraged after 2 weeks of no responses

Cold outreach is a numbers game. Your first 500 emails might get 5 replies and 0 clients. That’s normal. It takes most agencies 4-8 weeks to close Client #1. Keep going. The emails compound — someone who ignores Email 1 might reply to Email 3. Someone who says “not now” might come back in 60 days. Consistency beats talent.

The Automated Missed Call Audit (Your Sharpest Weapon)

Call a list of prospects, detect whether a human answers vs. voicemail, log the result, then open your outreach with: "We called your main line 3 times last Tuesday — twice it went to voicemail. Here's what that's costing you." This is buildable for pennies with Twilio's Answering Machine Detection (AMD).

  1. n8n triggers Twilio to dial a prospect's main number during business hours.
  2. Twilio AMD classifies the pickup: human, machine_start (voicemail), or no-answer (~94% accuracy, ~$0.0075 per answered call).
  3. Hang up immediately and log the result to Supabase. You never play audio or run a bot — it's a connectivity test, which keeps you clear of robocall rules.
  4. Prospects that missed your calls go to the top of your outreach list with their result quoted in the email.

Auditing 1,000 prospects costs roughly $15 total and produces hyper-specific proof. Respect calling windows (8am–9pm local), don't hammer the same number, and target main business lines, not personal cells.

⚠️ Legal: Do NOT use AI voice to cold-call prospects

In February 2024 the FCC ruled AI-generated voices are "artificial voices" under the TCPA. Cold-calling prospects with an AI voice requires prior written consent you don't have — penalties run $500–$1,500 per call. State "mini-TCPA" laws add more risk. Keep AI voice for inbound (your product) and consented calls only.

Compliant prospecting channels: cold email (legal under CAN-SPAM for B2B with truthful headers, a physical address, and a working opt-out), human-dialed follow-ups to business landlines, and the hang-up Missed Call Audit (measurement only, no message delivered).

2026 brought documented incidents: malicious skill packages on "ClawHub," leaked plaintext API keys, the "ClawJacked" hijack, and 20,000+ instances exposed on the open internet. Too risky for a business holding client phone access and PII.

🔐 Security rules if you run ANY autonomous agent

  • • Run it on an isolated VPS, never your main laptop or production server.
  • • Give it scoped, read-mostly access — never your live Vapi/Twilio/Stripe keys or client databases with write access.
  • • Never expose its control port to the internet. Most real-world breaches are misconfiguration, not exotic hacks.
  • • Treat community "skills/plugins" as untrusted code. Don't install random ones.
  • • Keep a human approving anything that contacts a client or moves money.
