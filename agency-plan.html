Overview: What You're Building

You're going to sell AI phone agents and automation to home service businesses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping). These businesses lose $45,000-$120,000 per year from missed calls. You fix that problem with an AI voice agent that answers 24/7, captures leads, and books appointments.

This is not a passive income play. It's a real service business with real clients who pay you $500-$2,000/month because you save them real money. The margins are 80-90% because the tools cost almost nothing per client.

The Niche: Home Services

Out of every niche researched, home services wins for a small team. Here's the cold data:

  • 500,000+ home service businesses in the US alone
  • Average HVAC company misses 30-40% of calls during peak season
  • 78% of callers who reach voicemail hang up and call a competitor
  • Each missed call = a $3,000-$10,000 job lost
  • Average annual loss from missed calls: $45,000-$120,000
  • Owners are non-technical — they want it done for them, not taught to them
  • Most are owner-operators who answer their own phone while on jobs
  • They understand ROI intuitively: "I pay you $500, I stop losing $5,000"

Services & Pricing: What You Sell

You sell three productized services. Fixed scope, fixed price. No custom quotes, no scope creep.

Service 1: AI Phone Agent

PRIMARY

An AI voice agent that answers every call 24/7, captures caller info, books appointments, and sends confirmations. The client's phone never goes to voicemail again.

Setup fee: $1,500-$3,000
Monthly retainer: $500-$1,000/mo
Your cost/client: ~$50-150/mo
Your margin: 80-90%

What the client gets: Dedicated phone number, custom-scripted AI agent, appointment booking, SMS confirmations, call logs dashboard, monthly performance report.

Service 2: Lead Follow-Up Automation

AI-powered SMS and email sequences that follow up with missed leads, send appointment reminders, and request Google reviews after completed jobs.

Setup fee: $500-$1,500
Monthly retainer: $300-$500/mo

Service 3: Review & Reputation Management

Automatically request Google reviews after jobs, respond to reviews with AI, and alert the owner to negative reviews instantly.

Setup fee: $500
Monthly retainer: $200-$300/mo

The "Never Miss a Call" Bundle (All 3)

Position this as the default. Most clients will choose the bundle because it's the complete solution.

Setup fee: $2,500 (saving them $500)
Monthly retainer: $1,000/mo (saving them $200)

The math for the client: They pay $1,000/mo. They were losing $4,000-$10,000/mo from missed calls. ROI is obvious.

Pricing Psychology

Always present three options (Service 1, Service 2, Bundle). Most choose the middle or bundle. Never charge hourly. You're selling an outcome (never miss a call), not your time.

Phase 1 — Setup

Week 1: Foundation

Both Together — 3-4 hours

Choose your agency name. Keep it simple: "[City] AI Solutions" or "[Your Last Name] Automation" works fine. Don't overthink this — you can always change it.

Register domain at Namecheap. Buy your agency domain (e.g., pittsautomation.com). Also buy 3 cold email sending domains (e.g., pittsauto-mail.com, pittsautomation-team.com, pitts-solutions.com). ~$10-12 each.

Set up Google Workspace on your agency domain. Create mike@youragency.com and paola@youragency.com. $6/user/month.

Set up Stripe account under your agency name. This is how you'll invoice clients.

Create a Carrd landing page. One page: headline ("We help HVAC companies stop losing jobs to missed calls"), 3 bullet points of what you do, a "Book a Demo" button linking to Cal.com, and your email. That's it. 30 minutes max.

Update both LinkedIn profiles. Headline: "I help HVAC/plumbing companies capture every call with AI | [Agency Name]". Add the agency as your current position.

DAY 2 Tech Account Setup

Mike — 4-5 hours

Spin up Hetzner VPS. Go to hetzner.com/cloud. Create a CX22 server (2 vCPU, 4GB RAM, $5/mo). Choose Ubuntu 22.04. Save the IP address and root password.

Install n8n on the VPS. SSH into your server and run:

sudo apt update && sudo apt install -y docker.io docker-compose mkdir n8n && cd n8n # Create docker-compose.yml and start n8n # Follow: https://docs.n8n.io/hosting/installation/docker/

Create Vapi account. Sign up, add a payment method. Get your API key from the dashboard.

Create Twilio account. Sign up, verify your phone number. Buy one local phone number ($1/mo). Get your Account SID and Auth Token.

Create Anthropic account. Get API key. Add $10 in credits to start.

Create Supabase project. Name it "agency-crm". You'll set up tables later.

Create Cal.com account. Set up two event types: "15-min Demo Call" (for prospects) and "Client Appointment" (template for client booking).

Create Resend account. Verify your agency domain for sending.

Create Loom account. Test recording a 30-second video.

Paola — 3-4 hours

Create Smartlead account. Sign up for Basic plan ($39/mo). Connect your 3 cold email sending domains. Start inbox warmup immediately — this takes 14 days, so start now.

Set up sending domains in Smartlead:

  1. Go to Namecheap → each sending domain → set up MX, SPF, DKIM, DMARC records (Smartlead gives you the exact records to add)
  2. Create a mailbox on each domain (e.g., mike@pittsauto-mail.com) using Google Workspace or a $1/mo Zoho Mail account
  3. Connect each mailbox to Smartlead
  4. Turn on warmup for all 3 accounts

Create Apollo.io account. Free tier. Set up your first search: Industry = "HVAC" OR "Plumbing", Employees = 1-50, Location = [your metro area], Title = "Owner" OR "President" OR "General Manager".

Export your first 200 leads from Apollo. Save as CSV. You'll upload these to Smartlead in Week 3.

Google Maps research. Search "HVAC companies near [your city]" and "plumbers near [your city]". Note the top 50 companies — their names, phone numbers, and whether they have a website. This is your manual call list.

DAYS 3-4 Learn the Stack

Mike — 8-10 hours over 2 days

n8n crash course. Watch these on the n8n YouTube channel (in order):

  1. "Getting Started with n8n" (basics of nodes, triggers, workflows)
  2. "HTTP Request node" (how to call APIs)
  3. "Webhook node" (how to receive data from external services)
  4. "AI Agent node" (how n8n handles AI-powered decisions)
  5. Search community workflows for "Vapi" or "voice agent" — clone and study them

Vapi crash course. Read the Vapi quickstart docs. Create your first test assistant. Make a test call. Understand: assistants, tools, phone numbers, and webhooks.

Connect Vapi to n8n. Create a simple workflow: Vapi webhook → n8n receives call data → logs it. This is the foundation of everything you'll build.

Paola — 4-5 hours over 2 days

Cold email crash course. Watch 2-3 YouTube videos on "cold email for agencies 2026" (channels: Alex Berman, Patrick Dang). Key concepts: open rates, reply rates, warmup, avoiding spam.

Write your cold email sequences. Draft all 3 emails in the sequence (see Scripts section below). Get them reviewed by Mike. Load them into Smartlead as a template (don't send yet — warmup still running).

Build your lead list to 500. Continue Apollo exports + Google Maps scraping. Organize in a spreadsheet: Name, Company, Email, Phone, City, Sub-niche (HVAC/Plumbing/Electrical).

Research 20 prospects deeply. Visit their websites, Google reviews, Facebook pages. Note: Do they have a "Contact Us" form or just a phone number? What are their hours? Do their Google reviews mention hard-to-reach or slow response? These become personalization hooks in your emails.

DAY 5 Build the Demo Voice Agent

Mike — 5-6 hours

Create a demo Vapi assistant for a fake HVAC company ("Comfort Pro HVAC"). Configure:

  • Greeting: "Thanks for calling Comfort Pro HVAC, this is the scheduling assistant. How can I help you today?"
  • The agent should collect: caller name, phone number, address, type of service needed (AC repair, heating, installation, maintenance), urgency level
  • If urgent: "I'll make sure a technician calls you back within 15 minutes."
  • If not urgent: offer to book an appointment (connect to Cal.com)
  • End: "Great, you're all set. You'll receive a text confirmation shortly. Is there anything else I can help with?"

Connect to Twilio phone number. Forward calls to your Twilio number to the Vapi assistant.

Build n8n post-call workflow:

  • Vapi fires webhook when call ends → n8n receives call data
  • Extract: caller name, phone, address, service type, urgency
  • Store in Supabase "leads" table
  • Send SMS confirmation to caller via Twilio
  • Send notification email to "business owner" via Resend

Test it end-to-end. Call the number from your cell phone. Pretend to be a homeowner with a broken AC. Verify: the AI agent handles the call, data arrives in Supabase, you get the notification, caller gets the SMS.

Paola — 2-3 hours

Call the demo number as different customer personas. Test edge cases: "I need someone RIGHT NOW, my pipes are bursting", "I just want a quote", "What are your hours?", "Can I speak to a real person?"

Note every issue — did the AI handle it well? Where did it stumble? Write down improvements for Mike to fix.

Continue building lead lists. Target: 500 total leads by end of Week 1.

  2. Show the lead appearing in the dashboard in real-time

Create a simple one-page PDF proposal (use Canva or Google Docs). Include: the problem (missed calls = lost revenue), the solution (AI phone agent), what's included, pricing (the 3 tiers), and a "Ready to start?" CTA.

Week 2: Build Follow-Up Automation + Prep Outreach

DAYS 8-9 Build Services 2 & 3

Mike — 8-10 hours over 2 days

Build the lead follow-up automation in n8n:

  • When a new lead is captured → wait 5 minutes → send SMS: "Hi [Name], thanks for calling [Business]. We've received your request for [service]. A technician will contact you shortly."
  • If no appointment booked within 2 hours → send follow-up SMS: "Hi [Name], just following up on your [service] request. Would you like to schedule a time? Reply YES and we'll set it up."
  • 24 hours before appointment → send reminder SMS
  • 1 hour after appointment time → send "How did it go?" SMS

Build the review request automation in n8n:

  • After "How did it go?" response is positive → wait 1 day → send SMS: "Glad we could help! Would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It really helps: [Google Review Link]"
  • If negative response → immediately email the business owner: "Alert: [Customer Name] reported a negative experience. Details: [response]. Follow up recommended."

Build a simple client dashboard. Use Supabase + a basic HTML page (or a Notion embed) showing: total calls this week, leads captured, appointments booked, reviews requested. This is what you show clients in their monthly report.

Paola — 6 hours over 2 days

Finalize cold email copy. Write 3 variations of each email in the sequence (A/B/C test). Load all into Smartlead.

Build a "hot prospects" list. From your 500 leads, identify 50 with the most pain signals:

  • Google reviews mentioning "couldn't get through" or "didn't answer"
  • Websites with only a phone number (no online booking)
  • Companies with 4+ star ratings but few recent reviews (need review automation)
  • Companies that answered YOUR call when you tested — they're the ones picking up while on jobs

Write voicemail script for the phone call campaign (see Scripts section).

Join 5-10 local Facebook groups: "[City] Small Business Owners", "[City] Home Services", "Contractors of [State]", etc. Don't post anything yet — just join and observe what they talk about.

Week 4: Close Your First Client

DAYS 22-28 Demo → Close → Onboard

The Demo Call Script (15-20 min)

Minutes 1-3: Build rapport

"Hey [Name], thanks for taking the time. Before I show you anything, tell me — how are you currently handling calls when you're out on a job?" Listen. Take notes. Mirror their frustration back to them.

Minutes 3-5: Quantify the pain

"Roughly how many calls would you say you miss in a week? And what's your average job value?" Do the math with them: "So if you're missing even 5 calls a week at $3,000 average, that's potentially $60,000/year walking out the door."

Minutes 5-10: Show the demo

"Let me show you what it looks like. I'm going to call this number right now, and I want you to listen." Call the demo number on speakerphone. Let them hear the AI handle a fake customer call. Then show the dashboard where the lead appeared.

Minutes 10-12: Show the full flow

"And here's the best part — after the call, the customer automatically gets a confirmation text. And you get an instant notification with all their info. No more playing phone tag."

Minutes 12-15: Handle questions

Answer their questions honestly. Common ones: "What if the caller wants a real person?" (it can transfer), "What about my existing phone number?" (we forward, no change needed), "What if they ask something the AI can't handle?" (it takes a message and alerts you immediately).

Minutes 15-18: Present pricing

"We have three options: just the phone agent at $500/month, or add the follow-up automation for $800, or the full package with review management for $1,000/month. Most of our clients go with the full package because it handles the entire customer journey. Setup is a one-time $2,500 fee, and we have you live within a week."

Minutes 18-20: Close

"Based on what you told me about missing [X] calls per week, this would pay for itself within the first week or two. Want to get started? I can send over the agreement today and have you live by [date]."

Test with the client on the line. Call together, let them hear it work with their business name. Get their approval.

Go live. Flip the switch. Send the client a "You're Live!" email with: their new phone number, a link to their dashboard, and your support contact.

What If You Haven't Closed Anyone by End of Week 4?

This is normal. Most agencies close their first client in weeks 3-6. If Week 4 ends with no client: (1) Review your email metrics — are people opening but not replying? Rewrite the body. Not opening? Rewrite subject lines. (2) Increase phone calls to 15-20/day. (3) Try a different angle — instead of cold outreach, offer 3 local businesses a free 2-week trial. Getting them to experience the product is more powerful than any pitch. (4) Don't panic. Keep the daily rhythm.

Phase 3 — Deliver & Grow

Your first client is your case study, your testimonial, your proof of concept. Over-deliver for them.

Internal FAQ (For You Two)

"Do we need an LLC?"

Not to start. Operate as a sole proprietorship or partnership to start. Once you're consistently earning $3K+/month, file an LLC ($50-500 depending on state). It's a 30-minute online form. Don't let this delay you.

"What if n8n or Vapi goes down?"

Set up monitoring (Mike builds this in Week 2). If your VPS goes down, restart it — takes 2 minutes via Hetzner dashboard. If Vapi has an outage, calls forward to the client's personal phone as backup. In 2+ years of operation, major outages are rare. Just have the fallback configured.

"What about client contracts?"

Use a simple service agreement. Google "SaaS service agreement template" and customize. Key terms: month-to-month (no long-term contracts — reduces friction), 30-day cancellation notice, setup fee non-refundable, you own the technology. Keep it to 1-2 pages.

"What if we get more clients than we can handle?"

Good problem. Each new client takes ~4-6 hours to onboard (after your first few). Ongoing maintenance is ~2 hours/client/month. At 20 clients = 40 hours/month of maintenance. That's very manageable for 2 people. Beyond 20, hire a part-time VA or bring on a technical contractor.
