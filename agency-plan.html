"Hey [Name], thanks for taking the time. Before I show you anything, tell me — how are you currently handling calls when you're out on a job?" Listen. Take notes. Mirror their frustration back to them.

Minutes 5-10: Show the demo

"Let me show you what it looks like. I'm going to call this number right now, and I want you to listen." Call the demo number on speakerphone. Let them hear the AI handle a fake customer call. Then show the dashboard where the lead appeared.

"And here's the best part — after the call, the customer automatically gets a confirmation text. And you get an instant notification with all their info. No more playing phone tag."

Minutes 12-15: Handle questions

Answer their questions honestly. Common ones: "What if the caller wants a real person?" (it can transfer), "What about my existing phone number?" (we forward, no change needed), "What if they ask something the AI can't handle?" (it takes a message and alerts you immediately).

Internal FAQ (For You Two)

"Do we need an LLC?"

Not to start. Operate as a sole proprietorship or partnership to start. Once you're consistently earning $3K+/month, file an LLC ($50-500 depending on state). It's a 30-minute online form. Don't let this delay you.

"What if n8n or Vapi goes down?"

Set up monitoring (Mike builds this in Week 2). If your VPS goes down, restart it — takes 2 minutes via Hetzner dashboard. If Vapi has an outage, calls forward to the client's personal phone as backup. In 2+ years of operation, major outages are rare. Just have the fallback configured.

"What about client contracts?"

Use a simple service agreement. Google "SaaS service agreement template" and customize. Key terms: month-to-month (no long-term contracts — reduces friction), 30-day cancellation notice, setup fee non-refundable, you own the technology. Keep it to 1-2 pages.

"What if we get more clients than we can handle?"

Good problem. Each new client takes ~4-6 hours to onboard (after your first few). Ongoing maintenance is ~2 hours/client/month. At 20 clients = 40 hours/month of maintenance. That's very manageable for 2 people. Beyond 20, hire a part-time VA or bring on a technical contractor.
